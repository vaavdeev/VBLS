# Paper1 — numbers and figures for the article on the permanent-trace principle

Script for the companion article (in preparation) that isolates the structural property behind the finiteness theorem of the monograph (Appendix A): a permanent record of damage exposure in the assimilation efficiency.

| File | Content |
|---|---|
| `paper1_numbers.py` | Integrates the exact vector field of the canonical system (Appendix A.1): the restoration term carries max(0, 1 − Σ), g(E) is clipped to [0, 1], and the state is not projected onto [0, 1]. Memory is written in kernel form, η = η₀·exp(−αH) with dH/dt = Σ − κH; κ = 0 is the permanent trace (K ≡ 1), κ > 0 an exponentially decaying kernel. |
| `paper1_numbers.out.txt` | Output of the last run, in seven sections: (1) the illustrative trajectory (t_B, death channel, branch times on the continuation, Σ_crit, the explicit E-channel bound); (2) the memory-sensitivity scan t_B(α) with both branches, the maximiser α*, and the case α = 0; (3) permanent versus decaying kernel in a constant environment, the explicit bound in that environment, and a damage-free start; (4) a trajectory that satisfies the conditions of Proposition 3 (start E(t_A) = 0.12), with the kink crossing, finite-difference slopes of t_B(α) at zero and the same slope from the variational system; (4a) Lemma 2 and Proposition 4: the fall time T₀ of the potential without inflow, in closed form and by quadrature, the bound on t_Bᴱ at every α, the explicit threshold ᾱ beyond which lifetime drops below its value at α = 0, and the approach of t_B(α) to T₀; for the set started at E(t_A) = 0.12, its T₀, its ᾱ and the rate of exit over a scan of [0, ᾱ], which checks the transversality condition of Proposition 4; (5) the same constant environment with Φ_cap alternating around its mean: the worst-phase condition and the resulting lifetimes; (6) two counterexamples: t_B(α) decreasing from α = 0 when restoration is not saturated (Section 6 of the paper), and a maximum of t_B inside the reserve branch, at a kink of the field (Supplementary Section S15), together with the check that this second example satisfies the conditions of Proposition 4: conditions (P1)–(P4) at α = 0, T₀, ᾱ and the rate of exit over a scan of [0, ᾱ]; (7) the integrator settings of every run (method, relative and absolute tolerance, maximal step), logged by the script from its own calls of the integrator: the paper takes its table of integrator settings from this block instead of transcribing them by hand. |
| `paper1_fig1.png` | (a) The illustrative trajectory and its continuation past t_B; (b) t_B(α) with the reserve and potential branches. |
| `paper1_fig2.png` | Permanent trace (K ≡ 1) versus exponentially decaying kernel (κ = 0.01) in a constant environment, on a logarithmic time axis: assimilation efficiency, potential and reserve; the death of the permanent-trace system is marked, and its continuation is dotted. |

**Run:** `python paper1_numbers.py` (Python 3 with NumPy, SciPy and Matplotlib; a few minutes). The figures are written next to the script.

**Parameters:** the illustrative parameter set of the monograph (Section 17.1); time in model units.

**Article 1_1.** Section 4a of the output supports Lemma 2 and Proposition 4 of the current revision of the article: these two statements are not in the monograph and, by the rule of the publication strategy, are not to be carried into it before the article appears.

**Related sections of the monograph:** Section 9.5 (the structural form of the theorem), Section 12.1.2 (the kernel dichotomy), Appendix A (Corollary 3, uniformity in the environment).
